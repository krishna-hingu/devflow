# STEP 1 — Product Definition

We are officially starting **Step 1 only**.

No UI design.
No architecture.
No folder structure.
No coding yet.

The purpose of this step is simply to answer:

> **What exactly is DevFlow, who is it for, and what problem are we solving?**

---

## 1.1 Product Name

# **DevFlow**

**Developer Productivity Workspace**

---

## 1.2 Product Type

DevFlow is a **frontend SaaS-style productivity/work-management application for individual developers**.

It combines:

```text
Projects
+
Tasks
+
Activity
+
Productivity insights
```

into one workspace.

---

## 1.3 Target User

### Primary user

**An individual software developer.**

Someone who may be working on:

* Personal projects
* Client projects
* Learning projects
* Portfolio projects
* Multiple tasks at the same time

They need a place to understand what they're currently working on and what needs to happen next.

---

## 1.4 The Problem

A developer working on multiple projects can easily lose track of:

```text
What projects am I working on?
What tasks are pending?
What should I work on next?
What have I completed?
What happened recently?
How is my work progressing?
```

DevFlow addresses this by putting those things into one workspace.

---

## 1.5 The Core Solution

DevFlow gives the developer a central workspace where they can:

```text
See their work
      ↓
Manage projects
      ↓
Manage tasks
      ↓
Track activity
      ↓
Understand progress
```

The important word here is **workspace**.

DevFlow shouldn't feel like five separate mini-apps.

The pieces should connect.

---

# 1.6 Core Product Promise

A concise product statement:

> **DevFlow helps developers organize their projects, manage their tasks, and understand their productivity from one focused workspace.**

That's the sentence we can use to judge future features.

For example:

### Feature idea: Task management

Does it help organize/manage work?

**Yes → relevant.**

### Feature idea: Random AI image generator

Does it contribute to the core product?

**No → don't add it.**

This gives us a **scope filter**.

---

# 1.7 Core Product Areas

At the product level, DevFlow has four areas:

```text
                 DEVFLOW
                    │
       ┌────────────┼────────────┐
       │            │            │
    WORKSPACE      WORK       INSIGHTS
       │            │            │
   Dashboard     Projects     Activity
                 Tasks        Analytics
```

And:

```text
              ACCOUNT
                 │
          Profile / Settings
```

These are **product areas**, not yet technical architecture.

---

# 1.8 What DevFlow Is NOT

This is equally important.

DevFlow is **not** currently intended to be:

* A team collaboration platform
* A Jira replacement
* A full project-management enterprise system
* A real-time communication application
* An AI coding assistant
* A billing/subscription platform
* A full-stack production SaaS
* Your ultimate portfolio flagship

Those could potentially become future directions, but they're outside our current product definition.

---

# 1.9 Product Scope

Our initial product boundary is:

```text
Developer
    ↓
DevFlow
    ↓
Dashboard
    ├── Projects
    │     └── Tasks
    │
    ├── Activity
    │
    ├── Analytics
    │
    └── Profile / Settings
```

That's enough product complexity to make this a serious React application without turning it into an enormous system.

---

# 1.10 Success Definition

For **DevFlow as a learning project**, success means:

> A developer can enter the application, understand their current work, navigate between projects, manage project tasks, inspect activity/progress, and use the application comfortably across desktop and mobile.

And for **you as the developer**, success means something slightly different:

> You can explain why the application is structured the way it is, how its data flows, where its state belongs, and how its React components work together.

That's the deeper goal.
